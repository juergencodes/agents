# Frame a Problem

Use this skill when the problem itself needs to be understood or framed before choosing or implementing a solution. This may be apparent from the outset or emerge during discussion or solution work.

## When to use

Consider problem framing when the problem or its relevant aspects are not yet understood well enough for the intended purpose, when framing the problem is itself challenging, or when a clear problem description would be a valuable standalone asset for later work.

Do not force a separate framing phase when the problem is already sufficiently clear for the task. The required depth depends on the agreed purpose and scope; do not imply that absolute completeness can be guaranteed.

## Approach

Read [guides/problem-framing.md](guides/problem-framing.md) for the detailed approach. Consult [examples/problem-frame.md](examples/problem-frame.md) when a concrete example would help.

The resulting frame may identify the problem, context, affected parties, goals, constraints, assumptions, challenges, and unresolved questions as appropriate. Use stable, unique IDs for aspects that may be referenced in later analysis, requirements, solution work, or progress tracking.

## Revisit the frame

Problem framing is not necessarily a one-time preliminary step. If solution work reveals a material misunderstanding, missing aspect, or changed premise, briefly explain the signal and its likely impact, then propose revisiting the relevant part of the frame. Do not silently continue as if the original framing were still reliable, and do not restart the entire framing unnecessarily.
