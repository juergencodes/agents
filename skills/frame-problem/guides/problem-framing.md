# Problem-Framing Guide

## Purpose and scope

Clarify the problem to the degree needed for its agreed purpose. Problem framing may itself be the requested deliverable, or it may prepare for requirements work or solution development. Keep these outcomes distinct: a problem frame explains what needs understanding; it does not prescribe a solution unless the user asks for one.

Agree or infer the scope from the request. Ask focused questions when an unresolved point would materially change the frame or subsequent work. Otherwise, state reasonable assumptions and mark them as provisional. Do not turn every unknown into a blocker.

## Useful aspects to examine

Select only what is relevant:

- The situation and context in which the problem occurs.
- The people, systems, or other parties affected.
- The desired outcome and how success might be recognized.
- Symptoms, underlying challenges, and relevant causes, distinguishing evidence from hypotheses.
- Constraints, dependencies, and boundaries of the problem.
- Assumptions, uncertainties, disagreements, and unanswered questions.
- Relevant interactions between subproblems or aspects.

Do not present an unverified cause as fact. Make material gaps visible and avoid false claims of completeness. A frame is sufficiently complete only relative to its stated purpose and scope.

## IDs and structure

Use concise, stable, unique IDs when aspects are likely to be referenced later, for example `P-1` for a problem aspect or `C-1` for a challenge. Choose a simple scheme that fits the artifact; do not assign IDs to every sentence or incidental observation.

Keep related aspects distinct where this supports later reasoning. Record relationships or dependencies when they matter. Preserve IDs while revising the same frame; explain replacements or merges if restructuring makes an ID obsolete.

## Output

Produce a readable, concise frame suitable for the intended audience. Clearly distinguish:

- established information;
- hypotheses or assumptions;
- open questions; and
- decisions about scope, if any.

If requirements are requested as the next deliverable, they may be developed from the frame, but do not silently conflate requirements with problem statements. A user may choose to stop with the problem frame as the final result.

## Reframing during solution work

When new evidence or reasoning suggests that the problem is materially misunderstood or incomplete:

1. Identify the new signal and the affected frame IDs or statements, if available.
2. Explain briefly why it could affect the current solution direction.
3. Recommend revisiting the relevant part of the frame, rather than automatically restarting all work.
4. Ask for clarification when needed; otherwise mark the revised understanding as provisional and continue only if appropriate.

Keep valid work where possible, but do not treat conclusions that depend on a superseded premise as settled.
